Why Documentation Drives Payment

Insurance companies pay claims based on documented evidence, not verbal descriptions. Every dollar you claim must be supported by a corresponding document. Claims that lack proper documentation are reduced, delayed, or denied — not because the damage is invalid, but because it is not provable on paper.

The Core Document Set

  • Complete insurance policy including declarations page and all endorsements
  • Written notice of loss (dated and sent certified mail)
  • Proof of loss — a sworn statement of your total claimed amount
  • Photographs of all damage (dated, organized by room or structure)
  • Room-by-room damage inventory with descriptions and estimated values
  • Independent contractor estimate with line-item scope of work
  • Receipts for emergency repairs and mitigation costs
  • ALE receipts if you are displaced (hotel, meals, storage)
  • Claim diary — a written log of every adjuster contact with dates and names
  • Any denial or reservation of rights letters from the insurer

Documents That Most Policyholders Miss

The most commonly omitted documents are the claim diary, the reservation of rights response, and the proof of loss. Each of these has a legal deadline attached to it. Missing the proof of loss deadline alone can void your entire claim in some states.

Proof of Loss

The proof of loss is a sworn statement that specifies exactly what you are claiming and for how much. It is not the same as the initial claim report. Most policies require it within 60 days of loss. Submitting a complete, detailed proof of loss supported by contractor estimates and photographs is the single most effective documentation move a policyholder can make.
