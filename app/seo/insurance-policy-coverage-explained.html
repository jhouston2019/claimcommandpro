The Standard HO-3 Policy

The HO-3 is the most common homeowners insurance policy in the United States. It covers your dwelling (Coverage A) on an open-perils basis — meaning all causes of loss are covered unless specifically excluded. Your personal property (Coverage C) is typically covered on a named-perils basis — only causes of loss specifically listed in the policy.

The Four Main Coverages

  • Coverage A — Dwelling: the structure of your home and attached structures
  • Coverage B — Other Structures: detached garage, fence, shed (typically 10% of Coverage A)
  • Coverage C — Personal Property: your belongings inside the home
  • Coverage D — Loss of Use / ALE: additional living expenses if your home is uninhabitable

What Is Almost Always Excluded

  • Flood damage (requires separate NFIP or private flood policy)
  • Earthquake (requires separate endorsement or policy)
  • Normal wear and tear or maintenance issues
  • Intentional acts
  • Ordinance or law upgrades — unless you have an Ordinance and Law endorsement

Hidden Coverages Most Policyholders Miss

Ordinance and Law coverage pays for the cost of bringing your home up to current building codes during a covered repair — a significant expense that many policyholders do not know they have. Matching coverage requires the insurer to replace an entire surface (roof, siding, flooring) when the damaged portion cannot be matched aesthetically. Extended replacement cost coverage adds 25–50% above your dwelling limit if construction costs exceed your coverage amount.

The most valuable coverages in a standard policy — Ordinance and Law, extended replacement cost, and matching clauses — are also the most commonly ignored by insurance adjusters. Knowing they exist is the first step to collecting them.

RCV vs ACV — Which Do You Have?

Replacement Cost Value (RCV) pays what it costs to replace damaged property with new materials of like kind and quality. Actual Cash Value (ACV) deducts depreciation — you get the current market value of a 10-year-old roof, not the cost to install a new one. If your policy is ACV, review whether endorsements convert any coverages to RCV.
