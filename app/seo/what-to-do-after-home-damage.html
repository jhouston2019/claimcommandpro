First Steps After Loss

What to Do in the First 48 Hours After Home Damage

The actions you take immediately after a loss have more impact on your final settlement than anything you do later. Here is the sequence that protects your claim.

Why the First 48 Hours Matter

Insurance claims are built on documented evidence. The first 48 hours are when the most evidence exists — before emergency repairs obscure damage, before contractors disturb the scene, before memory fades. Every hour you wait without documenting is an hour the insurer can use to dispute what happened.

Hour-by-Hour Sequence

  • Ensure safety first — do not enter a structurally compromised building
  • Photograph everything before touching or moving anything
  • Begin emergency mitigation to prevent further damage — this is required by your policy
  • Keep every receipt for emergency repairs, hotel stays, and meals if displaced
  • Call your insurance company to report the loss and get a claim number
  • Write down the date, time, cause, and exact description of the damage
  • Do not sign anything the insurer sends you without reading it first

The Documentation Rule

Photograph damage from multiple angles before any cleanup or repair begins. Include wide shots showing the full scope and close-up shots showing specific damage. Date every photograph. If possible, take a video walkthrough narrating what you see in each room.

Do not throw away damaged items before your adjuster inspects them. Disposing of damaged property — even obvious debris — can give the insurer grounds to dispute your claim.

Notify Your Insurer in Writing

Most policies require written notice of loss within a specific timeframe — typically 24 to 72 hours. Call to report the loss, then follow up with a written notice sent via certified mail the same day. The written notice creates a legal record of when you reported.

What Not to Do

  • Do not begin permanent repairs before the adjuster inspects
  • Do not give a recorded statement without understanding your rights
  • Do not accept the adjuster's estimate as final on the day of inspection
  • Do not sign a release, a proof of loss, or any settlement document on the day of loss
