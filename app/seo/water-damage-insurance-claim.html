Water Damage Insurance Claim — What's Covered and What to Do

Water damage is the most common homeowners insurance claim. It is also one of the most disputed. The cause of the water determines whether you are covered.

Start Free Analysis →

What Water Damage Is Covered

Standard homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental water damage — a burst pipe, an appliance failure, or rain entering through a storm-damaged roof. The key word is sudden. Gradual water damage from a slow leak, condensation, or long-term seepage is almost always excluded as a maintenance issue.

What Is Not Covered

  • Flood damage from rising water outside the home (requires separate flood policy)
  • Gradual leaks or seepage over time
  • Backup of sewers or drains unless you have a specific endorsement
  • Damage caused by failure to maintain the home

The First 72 Hours Are Critical

Water damage worsens rapidly. Mold can begin forming within 24 to 48 hours of a water event. Your policy requires you to mitigate — take reasonable steps to prevent further damage. Hire a mitigation contractor immediately, document their drying logs and equipment readings, and keep every receipt. Mitigation costs are typically covered.

Drying logs from your mitigation contractor — daily moisture readings and equipment placement records — are essential documentation for a water damage claim. Insurers use these to verify the scope and duration of drying required.

What to Document

  • Photograph all water damage before mitigation begins
  • Document the source of the water with photos and contractor notes
  • Keep all drying logs, moisture readings, and equipment inventories from mitigation
  • Get a written scope of repairs from a licensed contractor after drying is complete
  • Document all contents damaged by water with photos and descriptions

Common Dispute Points in Water Claims

  • Insurer claims damage is gradual, not sudden — dispute with contractor documentation of acute cause
  • Mold remediation omitted from estimate — required when drying was incomplete or delayed
  • Matching — insurer prices only damaged flooring sections when full room must match
  • Contents undervalued — document every item with age, brand, and replacement cost
