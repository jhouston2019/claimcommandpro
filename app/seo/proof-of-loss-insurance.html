What Is a Proof of Loss and How Do You File It?

The proof of loss is one of the most important documents in your claim. Most policyholders file it late, file it incomplete, or don't file it at all.

What a Proof of Loss Is

A proof of loss is a sworn, signed statement from the policyholder that specifies the date and cause of loss, the property damaged, and the total dollar amount being claimed. It is a formal legal document — not the initial claim report, not the adjuster's estimate, and not a simple letter to your insurer.

Why It Matters

The proof of loss is the policyholder's official statement of what they are claiming and for how much. Insurers use it to evaluate whether the claimed amount is supported by documentation. A complete proof of loss with contractor estimates and photographs attached is significantly harder to dispute than a verbal or informal submission.

Deadline Requirements

Most standard homeowners policies require the proof of loss to be submitted within 60 days of loss or within 60 days of the insurer's request. Some states impose their own deadlines by statute. Missing the deadline can give the insurer grounds to deny the claim entirely. Always confirm the deadline in your policy and submit before it — not on it.

Submit your proof of loss via certified mail with return receipt. The postmark is your legal evidence of timely filing. Keep the original signed copy.

What to Include

  • Date and cause of loss
  • Description of all damaged property with room-by-room detail
  • Total claimed amount supported by contractor estimates
  • List of all attached supporting documents
  • Your policy number and claim number
  • Signed and sworn (notarized in some states)

Common Mistakes

  • Submitting before completing your independent estimate (locks in a low number)
  • Filing without attaching contractor estimates and damage photos
  • Missing the deadline
  • Understating the total claimed amount to seem "reasonable"
  • Signing a proof of loss the insurer drafted without reviewing it
