What Counts as a Delay

A delay is not just waiting — it is the insurer failing to meet their own regulatory obligations. Every state requires insurers to acknowledge a claim within a specified number of days, begin investigation within a specified number of days, and issue a coverage determination within a specified number of days. When they miss these deadlines, they are in violation of state claim handling regulations.

Typical State Deadlines

  • Acknowledge receipt of claim: 10–15 days in most states
  • Begin investigation: 10 days in most states
  • Issue determination or request more information: 30–45 days in most states
  • Pay approved claim: 5–30 days after settlement agreement in most states

How to Document a Delay

Every contact with your insurer should be documented in writing. Keep a claim diary that records the date, the person you spoke with, and what was said or promised. Follow every phone call with a written email confirming what was discussed. This diary becomes your evidence if you need to file a regulatory complaint.

A delay complaint filed with your state's Department of Insurance is free, takes less than 30 minutes, and triggers a mandatory regulatory response. It is the single fastest way to get a stalled claim moving.

Your Options When a Claim Is Delayed

  • Send a written demand for status update with a specific response deadline
  • File a complaint with your state's Department of Insurance
  • Reference the specific state regulation your insurer has violated
  • Request escalation to the adjuster's supervisor in writing
  • Consult a public adjuster or attorney if delay exceeds 90 days
