The First Offer Is Not Final

Insurance companies expect negotiation. Their initial estimate is a documented opening position, not a final determination of what your claim is worth. Policyholders who respond with written, documented counter-proposals consistently recover more than those who accept the first number.

What Makes a Counter-Proposal Work

The most effective counter-proposals share three characteristics: they reference specific policy language requiring coverage, they attach an independent contractor estimate showing market-rate pricing for the disputed work, and they quantify the gap between the insurer's offer and the documented loss with precision.

A demand letter that says "your estimate is too low" without documentation is ignored. A demand letter that says "your estimate omits $6,200 in ridge vent replacement and applies a labor rate $18 per hour below local market" gets a response.

The Negotiation Sequence

  • Submit a written supplement request within 10 business days of receiving the low offer
  • Attach your independent contractor estimate with line-item pricing
  • Include a gap analysis showing exactly where the insurer's numbers differ
  • Reference the specific policy sections that require coverage of disputed items
  • Set a written response deadline — 10 business days is standard
  • If no response, send a follow-up referencing your state's claim handling regulations
  • If still unresolved, invoke the appraisal clause in your policy

When to Invoke Appraisal

The appraisal clause is a formal dispute mechanism in virtually every standard homeowners policy. Either party can invoke it when the insurer and policyholder cannot agree on the amount of loss. It bypasses litigation and typically resolves in 30 to 90 days. Invoking appraisal is most effective when you have a fully documented independent estimate that materially differs from the insurer's scope.
