Why Adjusters Miss Damage

Insurance adjusters inspect dozens of properties per week under time pressure. They miss damage for several reasons: the damage is not visible without removing materials, it falls outside the scope of what they were assigned to evaluate, their estimating software does not include the line item, or they simply did not look.

Hidden damage — water intrusion behind walls, structural movement, secondary damage from delayed mitigation — is almost never captured in a first inspection.

Your Right to a Supplement

Discovering additional damage after the initial estimate does not close your claim. You have the right to submit a supplement — a documented request to add missed items to the insurer's estimate. Supplements are standard in the insurance industry and should be submitted with photographic evidence and contractor documentation.

An adjuster's first estimate is not a final scope of loss. It is a snapshot of what they observed on a single visit. Supplementing with documented additional damage is your right under virtually every standard policy.

How to Document Missed Damage

  • Photograph all damage not reflected in the insurer's estimate
  • Get a contractor to include missed items in a revised or separate estimate
  • Create a written comparison showing what is in the insurer's estimate vs. what is missing
  • Reference photos with dates and location descriptions
  • Submit everything in writing via certified mail

Requesting Re-Inspection

If missed damage is significant, request a formal re-inspection in writing. Address it to your adjuster and copy their supervisor. Specify the items that were missed and attach your contractor's documentation. Set a 10-business-day response deadline.
