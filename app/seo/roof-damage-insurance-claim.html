How to File a Roof Damage Insurance Claim and Get Paid Correctly

Roof claims are the single most common property insurance claim — and the most commonly underpaid. Here's what carriers routinely miss and how to recover it.

Why Roof Claims Are Frequently Underpaid

Insurers routinely underpay roof claims by omitting required line items, applying excessive depreciation, ignoring matching requirements, and excluding code upgrade costs. On a full roof replacement, these omissions collectively reduce the claim by 20 to 35 percent in most markets.

What Carriers Routinely Omit on Roof Claims

  • Drip edge — required by current building codes in most states but frequently omitted
  • Ice and water shield — required at eaves and valleys in cold climates
  • Ridge vent — omitted when the existing vent was damaged and must be replaced
  • Overhead and profit — required on virtually all roof replacements involving a contractor
  • Permits — required for every permitted roof replacement in most jurisdictions
  • Decking replacement — carriers often allow for partial decking when full replacement is required
  • Matching — carriers price only the damaged slopes when the full roof must match

Depreciation on Roofing

Insurers apply depreciation to roofing materials based on age. A 15-year-old roof may receive 60 to 80 percent depreciation on ACV policies, leaving you with a fraction of replacement cost. If you have RCV coverage, you receive the depreciation holdback after completing repairs. Confirm your settlement type before accepting any payment.

Overhead and profit alone — typically 20% of the total estimate — is omitted from the majority of roof claim estimates. On a $20,000 roof replacement, that is $4,000 the insurer left out of their calculation.

How to Document Roof Damage

  • Photograph the full roof from the ground showing overall condition
  • Photograph damage to individual shingles, flashing, ridge cap, and vents
  • Get a contractor's written report identifying the cause and extent of damage
  • Request the insurer's estimate in writing and compare it line by line
  • Document all code upgrade requirements with your local building department
