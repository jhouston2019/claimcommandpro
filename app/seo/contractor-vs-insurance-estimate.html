The Estimate Gap Is Normal

It is standard for independent contractor estimates to exceed insurance company estimates by 20 to 40 percent. This gap exists because insurers use proprietary pricing software that applies labor rates, material costs, and overhead figures that do not reflect what licensed contractors in your region actually charge.

Why the Numbers Differ

  • Labor rates — insurer uses regional averages that undercut local market rates
  • Overhead and profit — insurer omits O&P that any general contractor must charge
  • Material pricing — insurer uses base pricing that excludes delivery, waste, and current market cost
  • Scope omissions — insurer excludes items the contractor considers necessary
  • Code upgrades — contractor includes required building code compliance; insurer often does not
  • Matching — contractor prices full surface replacement for uniformity; insurer prices only damaged sections

Which Number Is Right

Your contractor's estimate is based on what it actually costs to complete the work correctly. The insurer's estimate is based on what they are willing to pay. Neither number is automatically correct — but the gap between them is the starting point for your dispute.

How to Close the Gap

The most effective way to close the estimate gap is a written line-item comparison submitted as a supplement request. For each line where your contractor's pricing exceeds the insurer's, you document the variance, explain why the contractor's number reflects actual market cost, and request the difference.

Overhead and profit alone — a line item insurers routinely omit — typically adds 20% to the total estimate value on claims involving a general contractor. On a $30,000 claim, that is $6,000.
