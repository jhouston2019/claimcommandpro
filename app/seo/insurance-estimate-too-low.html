Why Insurance Estimates Come In Low

Insurance adjusters work for the insurer. Their job is to settle your claim at the lowest defensible number. Common reasons estimates come in low include missing line items, outdated pricing, labor rate suppression, omitted overhead and profit, and damage that was not physically inspected.

The gap between what the insurer offers and what contractors actually charge to complete the work is called the estimate gap. On disputed property claims, this gap averages $18,400.

Your Right to Dispute

Every standard homeowners policy includes an appraisal clause — a formal dispute mechanism that allows you to challenge the insurer's estimate with your own independent assessment. You do not need an attorney to invoke it.

Before invoking appraisal, most policyholders succeed by submitting a written supplement request backed by an independent contractor estimate and a line-item comparison showing where the insurer's numbers differ from market rates.

The insurer's adjuster is not the final word on what your claim is worth. A documented independent estimate with line-item variance analysis is the most effective first response to a low offer.

What a Line-Item Comparison Shows

A line-item comparison places the insurer's estimate and your contractor's estimate side by side. It identifies every line where the insurer used a lower unit price, omitted a required item, or applied depreciation incorrectly. Each gap becomes a documented dispute point.
