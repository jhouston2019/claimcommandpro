Filing Your Claim

How to File a Homeowners Insurance Claim — Step by Step

Filing correctly from the start determines how much you collect. Most policyholders make avoidable mistakes in the first week that cost them thousands.

Step 1 — Document Before You Report

Before you call your insurer, photograph every damaged area in detail. Create a written list of everything damaged with descriptions and estimated ages. This documentation becomes the foundation of your claim. Once you call and start the official process, the clock starts on multiple deadlines.

Step 2 — Report the Loss and Get a Claim Number

Call your insurer's claims line and report the loss. Give them the date, time, and cause of damage. Get your claim number in writing — you will need it for everything. Follow the call with a written notice sent via certified mail that same day.

Step 3 — Mitigate Further Damage

Your policy requires you to take reasonable steps to prevent additional damage after a loss. This means tarping a damaged roof, extracting standing water, or boarding broken windows. Keep every receipt — these costs are typically reimbursable as part of your claim.

Step 4 — Get an Independent Contractor Estimate

Before the adjuster's inspection, get at least one estimate from a licensed contractor who is not affiliated with your insurer. This estimate is your independent baseline. Do not share it with the adjuster before they complete their own estimate.

The adjuster's estimate and your contractor's estimate will almost certainly differ. The gap between them is the starting point for your claim negotiation — not the end of it.

Step 5 — Handle the Adjuster Inspection

Be present for the adjuster's inspection. Walk them through every area of damage. Point out items they miss. Do not sign anything during the inspection. After the inspection, send a written follow-up listing everything that was discussed and confirming any commitments made.

Step 6 — Review the Estimate Before Accepting It

The insurer's estimate will arrive within a few days of the inspection. Do not accept it as final. Compare it line by line to your contractor's estimate. Every variance is a documented dispute point.

Step 7 — Submit Your Proof of Loss

The proof of loss is your sworn statement of what you are claiming and for how much. It must be submitted within the deadline in your policy — typically 60 days from loss. Submit it certified mail. Never submit a proof of loss that understates your actual loss.
