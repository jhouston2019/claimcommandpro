What Is First Notice of Loss (FNOL)?

The first call to your insurer starts the clock on multiple deadlines. Here is what to say, what not to say, and why you need written notice the same day.

Definition
First notice of loss (FNOL) is the initial notification a policyholder makes to their insurer reporting that a covered event has occurred — starting the claims process and triggering regulatory deadlines for insurer response.

When to Make FNOL

Most policies require notice within a reasonable time after a loss — often interpreted as 24 to 72 hours. Some policies have explicit notice deadlines. Failing to provide timely notice can give the insurer grounds to deny the claim on procedural grounds. Report the loss as soon as it is safe to do so.

What to Say During FNOL

  • Date, time, and location of the loss
  • Cause of loss — fire, water, storm, etc.
  • General description of damage
  • Whether the property is habitable
  • Your contact information

What Not to Say During FNOL

  • Do not describe the full extent of damage in detail — you have not had time to assess everything
  • Do not give a recorded statement without understanding your rights
  • Do not speculate about cause if you are not certain
  • Do not accept or decline coverage determinations on the first call

Why Written Notice Matters

A phone call starts the process but does not create a documented legal record. Follow every FNOL phone call with a written notice sent via certified mail the same day. The written notice records the date, the loss description, and your contact information — and creates a legally documented record of timely reporting.

Get your claim number in writing before ending the first call. You will need it for every subsequent communication. If the representative cannot give you a claim number, ask for one in a follow-up email that day.
