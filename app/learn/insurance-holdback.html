Settlement Terms

What Is a Holdback in Insurance?

Your insurer is holding money from your settlement. Here is why, how much, and exactly what you need to submit to get it released.

Definition
A holdback is the portion of an RCV insurance settlement withheld until the policyholder completes repairs and submits proof — representing the difference between ACV and full replacement cost.

Why Insurers Withhold Funds

On RCV policies, the insurer pays ACV first — and holds the depreciation difference until repairs are complete. This protects against policyholders receiving full replacement cost without completing repairs. Once repairs are complete and documented, the holdback is released.

How Much Is Withheld

The holdback equals the total depreciation applied to your claim. On a $30,000 estimate with $8,000 in depreciation, your initial ACV check is $22,000 and your holdback is $8,000. After completing repairs and submitting receipts, you receive the $8,000.

How to Release the Holdback

  • Complete all repairs covered under the claim
  • Obtain signed contractor completion certificates
  • Compile all invoices and receipts
  • Submit to your insurer in writing via certified mail
  • Reference your claim number and request release of the depreciation holdback
  • Allow 15 to 30 days for processing

Holdback money does not expire immediately, but most policies set a deadline — typically 180 days to 2 years from the date of loss. Do not wait until the deadline is near to submit.
