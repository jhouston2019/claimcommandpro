Coverage Terms

What Is Loss of Use Coverage (Coverage D)?

If your home is uninhabitable, your policy likely pays your hotel, meals, storage, and other displacement costs. Here is how to claim every dollar.

Definition
Loss of use coverage (Coverage D) pays the additional living expenses you incur when your home is uninhabitable after a covered loss — hotel, meals above normal, storage, laundry, and other necessary costs.

What Coverage D Pays For

  • Hotel or rental housing while repairs are completed
  • Meals above your normal food budget — the additional cost of eating out because you cannot cook at home
  • Pet boarding if your temporary housing does not allow pets
  • Storage for belongings removed from the damaged property
  • Laundry costs if your temporary housing lacks facilities
  • Mileage for additional commuting caused by displacement
  • Any expense you would not have had if your home were habitable

What Coverage D Does Not Pay

Coverage D pays the additional cost of living — the difference between your normal living expenses and what you are spending because of displacement. It does not pay your normal grocery budget, your regular commute, or expenses you would have had regardless of the loss.

How to Document ALE Claims

  • Keep every receipt from the date of loss forward
  • Separate additional costs from normal costs — restaurants above your normal food spend, not all meals
  • Document the reason for each expense — hotel because home is uninhabitable, storage because items were removed for safety
  • Submit receipts monthly rather than waiting until repairs are complete

ALE claims are frequently undervalued because policyholders do not keep receipts or do not claim all eligible expenses. Every documented additional cost from displacement is claimable. The limit is your Coverage D limit — not a per-day cap unless your policy specifies one.
