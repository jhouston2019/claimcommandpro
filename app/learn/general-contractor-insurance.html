What Is a General Contractor in an Insurance Claim?

When your repair requires multiple trades, a general contractor is required — and their overhead and profit is a covered claim cost that insurers routinely skip.

Definition
A general contractor is a licensed professional who manages complex repair projects involving multiple subcontractors — coordinating scheduling, permits, inspections, and quality control — and whose management cost is compensated through overhead and profit.

When a General Contractor Is Required

Simple repairs — replacing a single window, painting one room — do not require a general contractor. Complex repairs involving multiple trades do. When your claim involves roofing, drywall, painting, electrical, and plumbing work simultaneously, a general contractor is required to coordinate the project. The three-trade rule is a common industry standard — three or more trades require general contractor coordination.

What General Contractors Do

  • Hire and schedule subcontractors for each trade
  • Obtain building permits and schedule inspections
  • Ensure work meets code and quality standards
  • Coordinate sequencing — roofing before drywall before painting
  • Take legal and financial responsibility for project completion
  • Interface with the insurer and adjuster on scope and payment

Why O&P Belongs in Your Claim

General contractor overhead and profit is a legitimate repair cost — not a markup or a bonus. When a homeowner hires a general contractor, they pay O&P. The insurer is required to cover legitimate repair costs. If a general contractor is required for your claim, O&P belongs in the estimate.

The insurer cannot require you to manage the repair project yourself to avoid paying O&P. If the scope of repairs requires a general contractor, the general contractor's management cost is a covered claim expense.
