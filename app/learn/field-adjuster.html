What Is a Field Adjuster in Insurance?

The field adjuster writes the estimate that determines your initial payment. Here is who they work for — and how to make sure nothing is missed during their inspection.

Definition
A field adjuster is a licensed professional who physically inspects damaged property on behalf of an insurance company and prepares the initial damage estimate and coverage determination.

Who the Field Adjuster Works For

The field adjuster — whether an insurer staff employee or an independent adjuster hired by the insurer — works for the insurance company. Their job is to assess the damage and price the repair at the lowest defensible cost. They are not your advocate. Understanding this dynamic is the foundation of effective claim management.

Types of Adjusters

  • Staff adjuster — employed directly by the insurer
  • Independent adjuster — a contractor hired by the insurer, typically during high-volume events like hurricanes and hail storms
  • Catastrophe adjuster — independent adjuster deployed specifically for major disaster claims

How to Handle the Inspection

  • Be present for the entire inspection
  • Walk the adjuster through every area of damage — do not assume they will find everything
  • Point out damage that may not be immediately visible
  • Take notes on everything the adjuster says
  • Do not sign anything during the inspection
  • Follow up in writing with a summary of what was discussed

The adjuster's first estimate is not final. It is their documented assessment of what they observed on a single visit. You have the right to supplement their scope with items they missed.
