What Is the Statute of Limitations on an Insurance Claim?

Miss the deadline to sue and you lose the right forever — regardless of how strong your claim is. Here is how long you have and when the clock starts.

Definition
The statute of limitations on an insurance claim is the deadline by which a policyholder must file a lawsuit against their insurer — after which the right to sue is permanently forfeited regardless of the merits of the claim.

How Long You Have

Most homeowners policies contain a suit limitation clause — typically one to two years from the date of loss. Some states override this with longer statutory periods. The shorter of the policy limitation and the state statute typically applies. Check your policy's suit limitation clause and your state's insurance statute.

What Starts the Clock

  • Most commonly — the date of loss
  • Some jurisdictions — the date of the insurer's final denial
  • Some policies — the date the loss becomes known

What Can Toll the Clock

  • Appraisal proceedings — some states toll the statute during appraisal
  • Written agreements to extend — get any extension in writing
  • Legal disability — minors or incapacitated parties may have extended deadlines

The statute of limitations is an absolute deadline. No court will hear your case after it expires regardless of how valid your claim is. Track this date from day one and consult an attorney well before it.
