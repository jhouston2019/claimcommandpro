Dispute Terms

What Is a Department of Insurance Complaint?

A free, 30-minute filing that triggers a mandatory insurer response. Here is when to file one and what happens next.

Definition
A Department of Insurance complaint is a formal regulatory filing with your state's insurance regulator alleging that an insurer has violated claim handling regulations — triggering a mandatory review and response.

When to File a DOI Complaint

  • Insurer exceeds regulatory timeframes for acknowledgment, investigation, or payment
  • Denial letter does not cite specific policy language
  • Insurer misrepresents policy provisions
  • Repeated requests for documentation already submitted
  • No response to written supplement requests within regulatory timeframes

What a DOI Complaint Does

Filing a complaint creates a regulatory record of the insurer's conduct and triggers a mandatory response from both the insurer and the Department of Insurance. Insurers take DOI complaints seriously — they are tracked, reported publicly, and can result in fines. Many stalled claims resolve within days of a DOI complaint being filed.

How to File

  • Find your state's Department of Insurance website
  • Complete the online complaint form
  • Attach your denial letter, your claim diary, and any relevant correspondence
  • Note specific dates and regulatory violations
  • File — it is free and takes less than 30 minutes

A DOI complaint is not a lawsuit and does not stop your statute of limitations clock. File the complaint and continue pursuing your claim through all other channels simultaneously.
