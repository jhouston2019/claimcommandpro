Estimate Terms

What Is a Line Item in an Insurance Estimate?

Every dollar in your claim is tied to a line item. Here is how to read them — and how each one becomes a dispute point when the numbers are wrong.

Definition
A line item is a single repair task or material in an insurance estimate, priced by unit (per square, per linear foot, per hour) — the atomic unit of claim negotiation.

What a Line Item Contains

  • Description — the specific repair task or material
  • Unit — how it is measured (square foot, linear foot, square, each)
  • Quantity — the measured amount
  • Unit price — the cost per unit
  • Total — quantity multiplied by unit price
  • Depreciation — reduction applied on ACV claims
  • Net total — total after depreciation

Why Line Items Matter

Every dollar in your claim flows through a line item. When the insurer's estimate is too low, the deficit exists at the line item level — a lower unit price, a wrong quantity, a missing item, or an improper depreciation deduction. Identifying which line items are wrong and by how much is the foundation of effective claim negotiation.

Common Line Item Problems

  • Missing items — damage visible in photos not reflected in any line item
  • Low unit prices — price per unit below current market rate
  • Wrong quantities — area measured incorrectly
  • Improper depreciation — non-depreciable items depreciated
  • Missing O&P — general contractor overhead not included as a line item

A line-item comparison is the most powerful document in a supplement request. It transforms a general complaint about a low estimate into a specific, quantified list of exactly what is wrong and by exactly how much.
