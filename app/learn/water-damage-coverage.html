What Is Water Damage Coverage in Homeowners Insurance?

Water damage coverage depends entirely on the source and cause. Here is what your policy covers — and what it doesn't.

Definition
Water damage coverage in a standard homeowners policy pays for sudden and accidental damage from internal water sources — burst pipes, appliance failures, and roof leaks from covered storm events — but excludes gradual leaks and external flooding.

What Is Covered

  • Burst or frozen pipes
  • Appliance water supply line failures
  • Water heater failures
  • Rain water entering through wind or hail-damaged openings
  • Roof leaks caused by covered storm damage
  • Overflow from plumbing fixtures due to sudden failure

What Is Not Covered

  • Gradual leaks — slow seepage over time is excluded as a maintenance issue
  • Flooding — rising water from outside the structure requires a separate flood insurance policy
  • Sewer and drain backup — excluded unless you have a water backup endorsement
  • Condensation and humidity damage
  • Neglect — damage worsened by failure to maintain or mitigate

The Sudden vs Gradual Line

The most common water damage dispute is the sudden-versus-gradual argument. Insurers sometimes reclassify sudden pipe failures as gradual deterioration to invoke the maintenance exclusion. A plumber's report documenting an acute failure — a specific fitting or joint that failed suddenly — is the counter.

Document the source of water intrusion with photographs and contractor notes before any repairs begin. Once the source is repaired, documenting the cause becomes significantly harder.
