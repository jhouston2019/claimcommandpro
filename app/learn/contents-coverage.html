Coverage Terms

What Is Contents Coverage (Coverage C)?

Most policyholders undervalue their personal property by 40 to 60 percent. Here is how contents coverage works and how to claim the full amount.

Definition
Contents coverage (Coverage C) pays to repair or replace your personal property — furniture, clothing, electronics, appliances, and other belongings — damaged or destroyed in a covered loss.

What Coverage C Covers

  • Furniture and household items
  • Clothing and personal effects
  • Electronics — computers, televisions, audio equipment
  • Appliances not built into the dwelling
  • Tools, sports equipment, and hobby items
  • Food and beverages destroyed in a covered loss
  • Items temporarily away from the home — in a vehicle, storage unit, or at college

Named Perils vs Open Perils for Contents

Unlike Coverage A which typically covers all perils except those excluded, Coverage C in most HO-3 policies covers only named perils — specific causes of loss listed in the policy. HO-5 policies cover contents on an open perils basis. Check your policy to confirm which perils are covered for your personal property.

Sublimits on Specific Items

Most policies include sublimits — lower limits within Coverage C — for specific categories of high-value items. Common sublimits include jewelry (typically $1,500), firearms, silverware, cash, and collectibles. If you own high-value items in these categories, you need a scheduled personal property endorsement for full coverage.

The most undervalued asset in a contents claim is clothing. Most households have $15,000 to $30,000 in clothing at replacement cost. Document every item by category — suits, shoes, outerwear, work clothes — before you accept a contents settlement.
