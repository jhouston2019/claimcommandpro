Coverage Terms

What Is Actual Cash Value (ACV)?

ACV pays less than repairs cost — sometimes far less. Here is how the calculation works and where insurers most commonly over-depreciate.

Definition
Actual cash value is the replacement cost of damaged property minus depreciation for age, wear, and obsolescence — representing the item's current market value rather than its replacement cost.

How ACV Is Calculated

ACV = Replacement Cost - Depreciation. Depreciation is calculated based on the item's expected useful life and its current age. A roof with a 20-year expected life that is 10 years old has 50 percent of its useful life remaining. On a $20,000 replacement cost, ACV would be $10,000. The insurer pays $10,000 — you pay the other $10,000.

Where ACV Depreciation Is Commonly Wrong

  • Non-depreciable items depreciated — labor, permits, and disposal are not depreciable under most policy language
  • Incorrect useful life applied — insurer uses shorter expected life than industry standards support
  • Depreciation applied to undamaged materials — only damaged portions should be depreciated
  • Blanket depreciation rate applied instead of item-by-item calculation

ACV vs RCV — Which Do You Have

Check your declarations page. If your policy says ACV for Coverage A or Coverage C, you are on a depreciated settlement. If it says RCV or Replacement Cost, you receive full replacement cost after completing repairs. Some policies are hybrid — RCV on the structure, ACV on contents.

Labor is not depreciable. If your insurer applied depreciation to labor costs in your ACV estimate, that is improper and disputable. Labor depreciation is a common carrier tactic that reduces claims by 10 to 20 percent.

Disputing ACV Depreciation

Request the insurer's depreciation schedule — the document showing how they calculated depreciation for each line item. Compare their useful life figures to industry standards. Any line where they applied depreciation to non-depreciable items or used an incorrect useful life is a documented dispute point.
