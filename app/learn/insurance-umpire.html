Dispute Terms

What Is an Umpire in Insurance Appraisal?

The umpire breaks ties between appraisers. Here is who they are, how they are selected, and what their decision means.

Definition
An umpire in the insurance appraisal process is a neutral third party jointly selected by the policyholder's appraiser and the insurer's appraiser to resolve disagreements about the amount of the loss — whose agreement with either appraiser produces a binding award.

The Umpire's Role

The umpire does not replace the appraisers — they resolve the dispute between them. Each appraiser submits their findings to the umpire. The umpire reviews both positions and agrees with one or the other — or reaches their own determination. Any two-of-three agreement (umpire plus either appraiser) is the binding award.

How the Umpire Is Selected

The two party-selected appraisers jointly select the umpire. Umpires are typically retired judges, experienced contractors, or insurance professionals. If the appraisers cannot agree on an umpire, either party can petition a court to appoint one.

The Umpire's Cost

The umpire's fee is split equally between the policyholder and the insurer. Umpire fees vary by location and claim complexity — typically $500 to $2,000 for each party.

The umpire does not advocate for either side — they evaluate the evidence submitted by both appraisers. The strength of your appraiser's documentation determines whether the umpire agrees with your position.
