Claim Process Terms

What Is a Claim Diary in an Insurance Claim?

A claim diary is your legal record of every commitment your insurer makes — and every one they break.

Definition
A claim diary is a written, dated log of every communication between a policyholder and their insurer during a claim — recording who was contacted, when, what was said, and what was promised.

Why a Claim Diary Matters

Verbal commitments disappear. When an adjuster says they will send a revised estimate by Friday and Friday passes with nothing, a claim diary entry from that conversation is your documented evidence. When a coverage determination contradicts something an adjuster said earlier, the diary entry is your counter. A claim diary transforms verbal conversations into a paper trail.

What Every Diary Entry Should Include

  • Date and time of the contact
  • Method — phone call, email, or in-person
  • Name and title of the person contacted
  • Their direct phone number and email
  • What was discussed — specific, not general
  • Any commitments made — by whom and by when
  • Any documents requested or provided

How to Maintain It

Record every contact the same day — memory fades quickly. After every phone call, send an email to the adjuster summarizing what was discussed and confirming any commitments. The email creates a written record of the phone conversation. Keep the diary in a single document that is easy to produce as evidence if needed.

A claim diary becomes essential in bad faith claims. If you need to file a regulatory complaint or pursue legal action, the diary is your chronological evidence of every interaction and every unfulfilled commitment.
