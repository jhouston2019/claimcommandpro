What Is Debris Removal Coverage?

Removing damaged materials is a real cost that belongs in your claim. Here is what debris removal covers and how to make sure it is not left out.

Definition
Debris removal coverage pays the cost of removing damaged property and materials from the loss site after a covered event — including demolished structural materials, fallen trees, and other covered debris.

What Debris Removal Covers

  • Removal of damaged roofing materials — tear-off and disposal of old shingles
  • Structural demolition debris from fire or storm damage
  • Fallen trees that damaged covered property — the tree removal cost
  • Water-damaged materials removed during mitigation
  • Contents removal in total loss situations

What Debris Removal Does Not Cover

  • Trees that fell but did not damage covered structures — typically limited or excluded
  • Pre-existing debris not related to the covered loss
  • Removal of undamaged property to access the repair area — covered separately as pack-out

Where It Appears in the Estimate

Debris removal appears as a line item in the estimate — typically as a tear-off or disposal line for roofing claims, or as a demolition and haul line for structural claims. If your estimate does not include a debris removal line and the scope requires it, it should be added as a supplement.

Roof tear-off and disposal is a required line item in every roof replacement estimate. If your insurer's estimate includes new shingle installation but no tear-off line, the tear-off cost was omitted and is supplementable.
