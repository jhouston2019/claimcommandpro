What Is Ice and Water Shield in a Roof Insurance Claim?

Ice and water shield is code-required in cold climates and frequently omitted from roof estimates. Here is what it is and how to claim it.

Definition
Ice and water shield is a self-adhering waterproof membrane installed at roof eaves, valleys, and penetrations to prevent water intrusion from ice dams and wind-driven rain — required by building code in cold climate zones.

What Ice and Water Shield Does

Ice and water shield protects the most vulnerable areas of a roof — eaves where ice dams form, valleys where water concentrates, and around penetrations like chimneys and skylights. It is self-adhering, forming a watertight seal that prevents water from infiltrating under shingles when ice dams push water upslope or wind drives rain under shingles.

Where It Is Required

The International Residential Code requires ice and water shield in climate zones where the average January temperature is 25°F or below. This covers most of the northern United States. Many states and local jurisdictions have extended this requirement to additional zones. When a roof is replaced as part of a covered claim, ice and water shield must be installed where required by code.

Why Adjusters Omit It

Like drip edge, ice and water shield is sometimes missing from adjuster scopes because it was not always code-required and Xactimate templates do not always include it. It is a code-required item in applicable climate zones and belongs in every roof estimate in those regions.

On a standard residential roof in a cold climate zone, ice and water shield installation costs $400 to $1,200 depending on roof size and complexity. If your insurer's estimate omits it and your home is in a qualifying climate zone, it is a supplementable item.
