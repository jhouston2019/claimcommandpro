Settlement Terms

What Is a Structured Settlement in Property Insurance?

Structured settlements spread payments over time. Here is when they appear in property claims and what to watch out for.

Definition
A structured settlement in property insurance is an agreement to pay a claim over a series of installments rather than in a single lump sum — sometimes used in large or complex property claims.

When Structured Settlements Appear

Structured settlements are more common in liability and personal injury claims than in property claims. In property insurance, they may appear in total loss situations, large commercial claims, or when the insurer proposes phased payment tied to repair milestones.

What to Evaluate

  • Total value — does the sum of all payments equal the full claim value
  • Timeline — when each payment is made and what triggers it
  • Default provisions — what happens if the insurer misses a payment
  • Interest — whether delayed payments include interest
  • Release language — whether each payment triggers a partial release

When a Lump Sum Is Better

For most property claims, a lump sum is preferable to structured payments. You need funds to pay contractors who do not wait for phased insurer payments. If the insurer proposes a structured payment tied to repair milestones, ensure the payment schedule matches your contractor's draw schedule.

Never accept a structured settlement without confirming the total value equals your full documented claim. Time value of money and contractor payment schedules make lump sum settlements generally preferable in property claims.
