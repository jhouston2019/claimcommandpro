Settlement Terms

ACV vs RCV — What Is the Difference in Insurance?

The settlement type in your policy determines how much you collect. Here is exactly how ACV and RCV differ — and how much it matters.

Definition
ACV (Actual Cash Value) pays replacement cost minus depreciation — the current market value of damaged property. RCV (Replacement Cost Value) pays the full cost to replace damaged property with new equivalent materials at current prices.

How ACV Works

ACV = Replacement Cost - Depreciation. A 10-year-old roof with a 20-year expected life is 50 percent depreciated. On a $20,000 replacement cost, ACV = $10,000. You receive $10,000 — and pay the other $10,000 out of pocket.

How RCV Works

RCV pays in two stages. First, the insurer pays ACV — the depreciated amount. After you complete repairs and submit proof, the insurer releases the depreciation holdback — bringing the total to full replacement cost. You must complete repairs to collect.

The Dollar Difference

  • 10-year-old roof, $20,000 replacement cost: ACV pays $10,000 / RCV pays $20,000
  • 15-year-old HVAC, $8,000 replacement cost: ACV pays $2,400 / RCV pays $8,000
  • 8-year-old flooring, $12,000 replacement cost: ACV pays $7,200 / RCV pays $12,000

How to Find Your Settlement Type

Check the declarations page. Each coverage line — Coverage A, Coverage C — shows its settlement type. Some policies are hybrid: RCV on the dwelling, ACV on contents. If it says ACV and you want RCV, ask your insurer about an RCV endorsement at renewal.

If you have RCV coverage and do not complete repairs, you forfeit the depreciation holdback and effectively receive ACV settlement. Complete repairs and submit for the holdback.
