Estimate Terms

What Is a Unit Price in an Insurance Estimate?

Unit prices determine how much the insurer pays per square foot, per linear foot, or per square of repair work. When they are wrong, the entire estimate is wrong.

Definition
A unit price is the dollar amount assigned to one unit of a repair item in an insurance estimate — multiplied by the quantity to produce the line item total.

How Unit Prices Work

Every line item in an insurance estimate has a unit price and a quantity. The total for that line is unit price multiplied by quantity. If the insurer uses a unit price of $4.50 per square foot for drywall installation and contractors in your area charge $6.00 per square foot, the unit price suppression on 500 square feet is $750 on that line item alone.

Where Unit Price Errors Occur

  • Labor unit prices below local market rates
  • Material unit prices below current supplier pricing
  • Specialty work priced at standard rates without difficulty adjustment
  • Post-disaster pricing not reflected in database rates

How to Document Unit Price Discrepancies

Obtain contractor quotes that show unit prices for the same line items. Where the contractor's unit price exceeds the insurer's, calculate the per-unit difference and multiply by the quantity to get the dollar impact on that line item. Compile all line items with unit price discrepancies into a variance table for your supplement request.

Unit price suppression is systematic — it affects every line item simultaneously. On a 500-line estimate with an average 15 percent unit price suppression, the cumulative impact can exceed the cost of the entire repair.
