What Are Additional Living Expenses (ALE) in Insurance?

If your home is uninhabitable, your policy likely pays your displacement costs. Here is what qualifies — and how much is available.

Definition
Additional living expenses (ALE) are the costs above your normal living expenses that you incur because your home is uninhabitable after a covered loss — covered under Coverage D in a standard homeowners policy.

What ALE Covers

  • Hotel or temporary rental housing
  • Restaurant meals above your normal food budget
  • Laundry costs if unavailable in temporary housing
  • Pet boarding if temporary housing does not allow pets
  • Storage for belongings from the damaged property
  • Mileage for additional commuting caused by displacement
  • Moving costs into and out of temporary housing

What ALE Does Not Cover

ALE covers the additional cost — the amount above what you would have spent if your home were habitable. It does not cover your normal grocery bill, your regular commute, or any expense you would have had regardless of displacement.

The Coverage D Limit

ALE payments are capped at your Coverage D limit — typically 20 to 30 percent of your Coverage A amount. On a $300,000 dwelling policy, Coverage D is $60,000 to $90,000. Track cumulative ALE expenses against this limit.

Submit ALE receipts monthly — do not wait until repairs are complete. Keep every receipt from the date of loss forward. The insurer will not reimburse undocumented expenses.
