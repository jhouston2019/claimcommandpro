What Is the Appraisal Clause in a Homeowners Policy?

The appraisal clause is one of the most powerful tools available to policyholders in a dispute — and most homeowners have never heard of it.

Definition
The appraisal clause is a provision in virtually every standard homeowners insurance policy that provides a formal, binding mechanism for resolving disputes about the amount of a covered loss — without litigation.

How the Appraisal Process Works

  • Either party — you or the insurer — can invoke the appraisal clause in writing
  • Each party selects a competent, independent appraiser
  • The two appraisers attempt to agree on the loss amount
  • If they cannot agree, they jointly select an umpire
  • Any agreement between two of the three parties (either appraiser and the umpire) is binding on both sides

When to Invoke Appraisal

Appraisal is most effective when the dispute is about the amount of loss — not about coverage. If the insurer agrees your loss is covered but disagrees on the dollar amount, appraisal resolves the dispute. If the insurer is disputing whether coverage applies at all, appraisal does not resolve the coverage question.

Cost and Timeline

Each party pays their own appraiser. The umpire's fee is split equally. Appraisal typically resolves in 30 to 90 days — significantly faster than litigation. The award is binding.

Invoking the appraisal clause signals to the insurer that you are serious about the dispute and have the documentation to support a higher number. Many claims that reach the appraisal threshold resolve in settlement negotiations before the process completes.
