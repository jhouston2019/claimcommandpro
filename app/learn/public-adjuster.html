What Is a Public Adjuster?

A public adjuster works for you — not the insurer. Here is what they do, what they cost, and when Claim Command Pro is a better option.

Definition
A public adjuster is a licensed insurance professional who represents policyholders in preparing, filing, and negotiating insurance claims — typically for a fee of 10 to 15 percent of the final settlement amount.

What Public Adjusters Do

  • Inspect and document the full scope of damage
  • Prepare a complete claim package — inventory, estimates, and correspondence
  • Negotiate with the insurer on your behalf
  • Identify coverages and endorsements the insurer may have missed
  • Handle all written communication with the insurer

What Public Adjusters Cost

Public adjusters typically charge 10 to 15 percent of the final settlement. On a $50,000 claim, that is $5,000 to $7,500. On a $100,000 claim, it is $10,000 to $15,000. The fee is often negotiable and is sometimes capped by state regulation. Most public adjusters work on contingency — no recovery, no fee.

When a Public Adjuster Makes Sense

  • Total loss claims where the claim value justifies the percentage fee
  • Complex commercial property claims
  • Claims involving significant disputes that require professional representation
  • Situations where you are unable to manage the claim process personally
