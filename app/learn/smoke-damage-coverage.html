What Is Smoke Damage Coverage in Insurance?

Smoke damage extends far beyond the burn area. Here is what it covers and why it requires its own separate estimate.

Definition
Smoke damage coverage pays for cleaning, deodorization, and replacement of materials in areas affected by smoke and soot from a covered fire event — a separate scope from direct fire damage that often extends throughout the structure.

How Smoke Damage Differs from Fire Damage

Direct fire damage burns and chars materials — replacement is obvious. Smoke and soot penetrate throughout a structure — into HVAC systems, wall cavities, attics, and finished surfaces in rooms far from the origin of the fire. Smoke damage requires assessment throughout the structure, not just in the burn area.

What Smoke Damage Coverage Pays For

  • Professional cleaning of all smoke-affected surfaces
  • HVAC system cleaning and filter replacement
  • Duct cleaning and deodorization
  • Replacement of materials that cannot be fully cleaned — drywall, insulation, carpeting
  • Content cleaning or replacement for smoke-affected belongings
  • Ozone treatment or thermal fogging for odor remediation

Why Adjusters Underscope Smoke Damage

Adjusters typically focus on the origin area of a fire. Smoke migration throughout the structure is often not fully assessed in a first inspection. A thorough contractor assessment of the entire structure is necessary to capture the full smoke damage scope.

HVAC systems can distribute smoke throughout a structure during a fire. Duct cleaning is a standard smoke damage line item that is frequently omitted from insurance estimates. If your fire involved the HVAC system running, include duct cleaning in your supplement.
