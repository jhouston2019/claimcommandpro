What Is Code Upgrade Coverage in Insurance?

When repairs require bringing your home up to current building codes, those upgrade costs are often covered — and often omitted by adjusters.

Definition
Code upgrade coverage pays the additional cost of meeting current building codes during repair of covered damage — costs that exceed what simple like-for-like replacement would require.

What Triggers Code Upgrades

Building codes change over time. When covered damage requires repair or replacement, the work must meet current codes — not the codes in effect when the home was built. This creates upgrade costs that go beyond simple replacement. Examples include drip edge requirements added to roofing codes, updated electrical panel standards, improved fire blocking requirements, and accessibility updates required by local ordinance.

Common Code Upgrades in Property Claims

  • Roofing — drip edge, ice and water shield, ridge ventilation, and specific underlayment types now required by code
  • Electrical — panel upgrades, AFCI breakers, and GFCI outlets required in renovated areas
  • Plumbing — updated pipe materials in remodeled areas
  • Framing — hurricane straps and seismic connections in code-updated regions
  • Accessibility — ADA requirements triggered by renovation threshold in some jurisdictions

Coverage Source

Code upgrade costs are covered under two possible policy provisions — ordinance and law coverage (an endorsement) or the broader replacement cost provision in some policies. Check your declarations page for ordinance and law coverage with a stated limit. Without it, code upgrade costs may not be covered.

Drip edge is required by building code in most states for any new or replacement roof installation. It costs $200 to $500 on a typical roof. Adjusters omit it in the majority of roof claim estimates. If your policy has ordinance and law coverage, drip edge is claimable.
