Settlement Terms

What Is a Lowball Insurance Settlement Offer?

A lowball offer is not a final answer — it is an opening position. Here is how to recognize one and respond with documented evidence.

Definition
A lowball offer is an insurance settlement offer that materially understates the documented value of the covered loss — typically based on suppressed pricing, omitted scope, or ignored coverage.

How to Recognize a Lowball Offer

  • The offer is significantly below your independent contractor estimate
  • The estimate omits line items your contractor considers standard
  • O&P is not included
  • Coverage endorsements are not reflected
  • The pricing uses unit rates below what contractors quote

Why Insurers Make Lowball Offers

Insurance companies make lowball offers because many policyholders accept them. Without an independent contractor estimate and a documented line-item comparison, most policyholders have no basis to challenge the number. Lowball offers are a systematic business practice — not a mistake.

How to Respond

  • Do not accept or reject verbally
  • Request the full estimate in writing if you do not have it
  • Get an independent contractor estimate immediately
  • Run a line-item comparison to document the gap
  • Submit a written supplement request or demand letter with documentation attached

The insurer's offer is their opening position. Your documented contractor estimate is your counter. The gap between them is the starting point for negotiation — not the end.
