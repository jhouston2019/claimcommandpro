Coverage Terms

What Is Recoverable Depreciation?

If you have RCV coverage, money was withheld from your first check. Here is how to get it back — and what you need to submit.

Definition
Recoverable depreciation is the amount withheld from your initial ACV payment on an RCV policy that you can recover by completing repairs and submitting proof to your insurer.

How the Two-Payment System Works

When you have replacement cost value coverage, your insurer pays in two stages. The first payment is the actual cash value — replacement cost minus depreciation. The second payment — the depreciation holdback — is released after you complete repairs and submit proof. The sum of both payments equals the full replacement cost.

How to Collect Recoverable Depreciation

  • Complete all repairs covered under the claim
  • Obtain signed completion certificates from your contractor
  • Save every invoice and receipt from every contractor involved
  • Submit proof of completion to your insurer in writing via certified mail
  • Include the invoices, receipts, and completion certificates
  • Reference your claim number and request release of the depreciation holdback
  • Most insurers release payment within 30 days of receiving complete documentation

Deadlines for Claiming It

Most policies set a deadline for submitting proof of completion — typically 180 days to 2 years from the date of loss. Check your policy for the specific timeframe. Missing this deadline forfeits the depreciation holdback permanently.

Many policyholders complete repairs and never submit for the depreciation holdback — leaving thousands of dollars uncollected. The insurer will not remind you. It is your responsibility to submit.

What If Repairs Are Not Complete

If you cannot complete all repairs — due to contractor availability, financing, or ongoing damage — you can submit for partial completion on completed portions. Document what has been completed and submit for that portion. You can submit for the remainder when additional work is complete.
