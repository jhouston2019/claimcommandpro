What Is a Mortgage Clause in an Insurance Policy?

If you have a mortgage, your lender is on your insurance check. Here is what that means and how to handle it correctly.

Definition
A mortgage clause is a provision in a homeowners insurance policy that protects the mortgage lender's financial interest by requiring the insurer to name the lender on claim payments and notify the lender of policy cancellation.

Why Lenders Are Named on Claims

When you have a mortgage, your lender has a financial interest in your property — they are owed money secured by it. If your home is damaged and the insurer pays the claim, the lender wants to ensure the money is used to restore the collateral securing their loan. The mortgage clause requires the insurer to include the lender as a co-payee on any significant claim payment.

How It Affects Your Claim Payment

  • Checks above a certain threshold (typically $10,000 to $20,000) are issued jointly to you and your lender
  • Both signatures are required to cash or deposit the check
  • Lenders typically require the funds to be held in an escrow account and released as repairs progress
  • Small checks may be issued to you alone depending on your mortgage agreement

How to Handle a Joint Check

Contact your lender as soon as you receive a claim payment check. They will direct you to their loss draft department. The process typically requires endorsing the check, submitting documentation of the damage and repair plans, and having funds held in escrow until repairs are complete and inspected.

Do not attempt to cash a check made out to both you and your lender without the lender's signature. It will be returned and may create complications with your claim. Contact your lender's loss draft department immediately.
