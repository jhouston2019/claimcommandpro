What Is Overhead and Profit (O&P) in an Insurance Claim?

O&P is the single most commonly omitted line item in insurance estimates. On a $30,000 claim, that omission costs you $6,000. Here is how to get it included.

Definition
Overhead and profit (O&P) is the compensation paid to a general contractor for managing a repair project — typically 20 percent of the total estimate — and is required on virtually all claims involving a general contractor.

What O&P Pays For

When a general contractor manages your repair, they coordinate subcontractors, obtain permits, schedule inspections, and take responsibility for the quality and completion of the work. This project management has a cost. The industry standard is 10 percent overhead and 10 percent profit — totaling 20 percent of the direct repair cost.

When O&P Is Required

O&P is required whenever a general contractor is needed to coordinate the repair. The three-trade rule is a common standard — when three or more trades are required (roofing, drywall, painting, electrical, etc.), a general contractor is typically necessary and O&P applies. Insurers frequently omit O&P and then contest its inclusion when challenged.

How Much O&P Adds to a Claim

  • $20,000 repair — O&P adds $4,000
  • $30,000 repair — O&P adds $6,000
  • $50,000 repair — O&P adds $10,000
  • $100,000 repair — O&P adds $20,000

O&P is not a markup or a bonus. It is the cost of professional project management that any homeowner hiring a general contractor will pay. When the insurer omits it, they are asking you to absorb a legitimate repair cost out of pocket.

How to Claim O&P

If O&P is not included in your insurer's estimate and you are hiring a general contractor, submit a supplement request citing the number of trades involved and the contractor's O&P requirement. Include your contractor's estimate showing O&P as a line item. The insurer cannot deny O&P when a general contractor is demonstrably required.
