Dispute Terms

What Is a Policyholder's Bill of Rights?

Some states have codified your rights during a claim. Here is what those rights typically include and how to use them if they exist in your state.

Definition
A Policyholder's Bill of Rights is a state statute or regulation enumerating specific rights that insurance policyholders are guaranteed during the claims process — including the right to prompt payment, clear explanations, and fair treatment.

Common Rights Included

  • The right to prompt acknowledgment of your claim within a specified number of days
  • The right to a clear, written explanation of any denial
  • The right to a prompt, fair, and equitable settlement
  • The right to an itemized explanation of any payment or partial payment
  • The right to have your claim investigated promptly and thoroughly
  • The right to appeal a denial
  • The right to file a complaint with the Department of Insurance

State Variation

Not every state has a formal Policyholder's Bill of Rights. However, virtually every state has claim handling regulations that provide similar protections under different names. Check your state's Department of Insurance website for applicable policyholder protections.

How to Use These Rights

When an insurer violates a specific right — fails to acknowledge your claim within the required period, issues a denial without specific policy citation — cite the specific right violated in your written response and in any DOI complaint.

Knowing your rights before a dispute begins gives you a vocabulary the insurer understands. A letter citing the specific regulatory provision violated gets a different response than a general complaint.
