Dispute Terms

What Is the Appraisal Process in Insurance?

Step by step — from invoking the clause to receiving the binding award. Here is exactly what happens.

Definition
The insurance appraisal process is the formal mechanism for resolving disputes about the amount of a covered loss — involving two independent appraisers and a neutral umpire whose combined decision is binding.

Step-by-Step Appraisal Process

  • Step 1 — Either party invokes the appraisal clause in writing
  • Step 2 — Each party selects a competent independent appraiser within the timeframe specified in the policy (typically 20 days)
  • Step 3 — The two appraisers attempt to agree on the amount of loss
  • Step 4 — If they cannot agree, they jointly select a neutral umpire
  • Step 5 — Each appraiser submits their findings to the umpire
  • Step 6 — Any agreement between two of the three parties is binding on both the policyholder and the insurer

Who Pays for Appraisal

Each party pays their own appraiser. The umpire's fee is split equally. Total cost varies by claim size and location but is typically $1,500 to $5,000 total — significantly less than litigation.

How Long It Takes

Most appraisals resolve in 30 to 90 days from invocation to award. Complex claims or difficulty selecting an umpire can extend the timeline.

Invoking appraisal before you have strong documentation weakens your position. Build your complete contractor estimate and gap analysis before invoking — your appraiser needs this foundation to advocate for your number.
