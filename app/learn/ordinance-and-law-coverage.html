Coverage Terms

What Is Ordinance and Law Coverage?

One of the most valuable coverages in a standard homeowners policy — and one of the most commonly ignored by insurance adjusters.

Definition
Ordinance and law coverage pays the cost of bringing your home up to current building codes when a covered loss requires repair or reconstruction.

Why Code Upgrades Cost Money

Building codes change over time. A home built in 1985 was built to 1985 codes. When that home is damaged and repaired in 2025, the repair must meet 2025 codes — which often require upgraded electrical panels, new ventilation standards, improved fire blocking, and other improvements that did not exist when the home was built. These upgrades are not optional. Contractors cannot legally complete the repair without them.

What Ordinance and Law Coverage Pays For

  • Demolition of undamaged portions of the structure when required by code to complete the repair
  • Cost to rebuild the demolished portion to current code standards
  • Cost of code-required upgrades to the damaged portion being repaired
  • Electrical, plumbing, HVAC, and structural upgrades required by the permit process

Why Adjusters Frequently Omit It

Ordinance and law coverage requires the adjuster to research current building codes for your jurisdiction and apply them to your specific repair scope. This takes time and expertise most field adjusters do not spend on standard claims. The coverage is often omitted not because it does not apply but because it was not evaluated.

On a roof replacement in most states, current building codes require drip edge, specific underlayment, and ventilation standards. If your policy has ordinance and law coverage and the adjuster omitted these items, they owe you the difference.

How to Confirm You Have It

Check your policy's declarations page for a line item labeled "Ordinance and Law," "Building Ordinance," or "Code Upgrade Coverage." It may also appear as an endorsement. The limit is typically expressed as a percentage of your dwelling coverage — commonly 10 to 50 percent.

How to Claim It

Document every code-required upgrade with a written statement from your contractor specifying which code section requires the upgrade and the cost. Submit this documentation with your supplement request. The insurer cannot deny coverage for a code-required upgrade if the coverage exists in your policy.
