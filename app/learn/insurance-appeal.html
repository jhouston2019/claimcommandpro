Dispute Terms

What Is an Insurance Claim Appeal?

An appeal is your documented counter to the insurer's position. Here is what makes one effective and how to submit it correctly.

Definition
An insurance claim appeal is a formal written challenge to an insurer's denial or underpayment determination — presenting documentation and policy arguments that contradict the insurer's stated position.

What an Effective Appeal Contains

  • Reference to the specific denial letter being appealed by date
  • Point-by-point rebuttal of each denial basis
  • Policy language contradicting the insurer's interpretation
  • Documentation supporting each rebuttal — photos, contractor reports, expert opinions
  • Demand for payment within 10 business days
  • Reference to regulatory complaints and appraisal rights if not resolved

Appeal vs Supplement

An appeal challenges a denial — the insurer said the loss is not covered. A supplement adds items to an accepted estimate — the insurer agreed coverage applies but missed items. Use the right document for the right situation.

How to Submit

Certified mail with return receipt to the adjuster and their supervisor simultaneously. Keep copies of everything. Set a calendar reminder for the response deadline.

An appeal that simply says you disagree is not effective. An appeal that identifies the specific exclusion misapplication, cites the correct policy language, and attaches contractor documentation of the cause of loss is effective.
