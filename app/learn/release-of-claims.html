Settlement Terms

What Is a Release of Claims in Insurance?

Once you sign a release, your claim is closed. Here is what the document says, what rights it waives, and what you must verify before signing.

Definition
A release of claims is a legal document signed by the policyholder that accepts a settlement payment in full satisfaction of the insurance claim — waiving the right to make further claims for the same loss.

What You Waive When You Sign

  • The right to supplement the claim for additional damage from the same event
  • The right to dispute the settlement amount
  • The right to file a bad faith claim related to the handling of this claim
  • Future claims for damage discovered after signing that arose from the same loss

What to Verify Before Signing

  • The settlement amount matches your documented and agreed loss
  • All covered items are included — structure, contents, ALE separately confirmed
  • RCV holdback process is documented if repairs are not yet complete
  • The release does not include future unknown damage that could not have been discovered
  • You understand every waiver in the document

Partial Releases

Some insurers ask you to sign a partial release — accepting payment for resolved portions of a claim while reserving rights on disputed portions. Read any partial release carefully to confirm exactly which portions are being released and which remain open.

Never sign a release the day the insurer presents it. Take it home, read every word, and confirm the settlement amount covers your full documented loss before signing. You cannot reopen a released claim.
