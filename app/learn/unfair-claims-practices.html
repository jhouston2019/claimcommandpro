What Are Unfair Claims Practices in Insurance?

Every state prohibits specific insurer behaviors. Here is what those behaviors are — and what to do when your insurer engages in them.

Definition
Unfair claims practices are specific insurer behaviors prohibited by state insurance regulations — including misrepresentation, unreasonable delays, and failure to properly investigate or pay covered claims.

Common Unfair Claims Practices

  • Misrepresenting policy provisions to avoid paying a claim
  • Failing to acknowledge or investigate a claim within regulatory timeframes
  • Refusing to pay a claim without conducting a reasonable investigation
  • Failing to affirm or deny coverage within a reasonable time
  • Compelling policyholders to litigate by offering far less than what is owed
  • Attempting to settle a claim for less than reasonable based on an insurer advertisement
  • Failing to respond promptly to communications

Where These Standards Come From

Most states have adopted some version of the NAIC Model Unfair Claims Settlement Practices Act, which defines prohibited practices. Your state's specific regulations can be found on the Department of Insurance website.

How to Report Violations

File a complaint with your state's Department of Insurance documenting the specific regulatory violation, the dates it occurred, and supporting evidence. DOI complaints are free, create a regulatory record, and trigger mandatory responses.

Document every potential unfair practice with dates, names, and specific conduct. Regulatory complaints require specifics — a general statement that the insurer treated you unfairly is not sufficient.
