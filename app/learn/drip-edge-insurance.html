What Is Drip Edge and Why Is It in an Insurance Claim?

Drip edge is required by building code on virtually every roof replacement. Insurers omit it on the majority of roof estimates. Here is why it belongs in your claim.

Definition
Drip edge is a metal strip installed along the eaves and rakes of a roof to direct water away from the fascia and into the gutters — required by current building codes in most states for any new roof installation.

Why Drip Edge Is Required

The 2012 and 2015 International Residential Code — adopted by most states — requires drip edge on all new roof installations. When your roof is replaced as part of an insurance claim, the installation must meet current code. Drip edge is not optional — contractors cannot legally install a code-compliant roof without it.

Why Adjusters Omit It

Drip edge was not always code-required. Xactimate databases and adjuster training materials sometimes lag behind current code adoption. Many adjusters omit drip edge from roof estimates because it was not in their standard scope template — not because it is not required.

What Drip Edge Costs

Drip edge installation on a standard residential roof costs $200 to $600 depending on roof perimeter and material type. On a $20,000 roof replacement claim, omitting drip edge costs you 1 to 3 percent of total claim value.

If your insurer's roof estimate does not include a line item for drip edge, submit a supplement request citing the applicable building code section for your state and your contractor's drip edge line item. This is a code-required item and is not discretionary.
