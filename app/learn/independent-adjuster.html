Claim Process Terms

What Is an Independent Adjuster?

Independent adjusters are contractors hired by insurers to handle surge volumes. Here is what that means for your claim — and why it matters.

Definition
An independent adjuster is a licensed claims professional who works as an independent contractor hired by insurance companies to handle claims — typically during high-volume periods like major storms and catastrophes.

How Independent Adjusters Differ from Staff Adjusters

Staff adjusters are employees of the insurer. Independent adjusters are contractors — they may work for multiple insurers and are typically paid per claim. During major catastrophes, insurers deploy independent adjusters to handle claim volume that exceeds their staff capacity. Independent adjusters generally handle claims under the same standards as staff adjusters but may have less familiarity with the specific insurer's policies and internal escalation process.

What to Know When Your Adjuster Is Independent

  • Get the adjuster's name, license number, and the name of their adjusting firm
  • Confirm they are authorized to make coverage decisions on your claim
  • Find out the name of the insurer's internal claims contact overseeing the claim
  • All communications still go to the insurer — not just the independent adjuster

Why It Matters

Independent adjusters handling high claim volumes after major events often spend less time per inspection than staff adjusters. This increases the likelihood of missed damage. Having your own contractor present and submitting a thorough supplement request is more important, not less, when an independent adjuster handles your claim.

If your adjuster changes midway through your claim — common when independent adjusters rotate off catastrophe deployments — get the new adjuster's information in writing and resend any outstanding supplement requests to the new contact.
