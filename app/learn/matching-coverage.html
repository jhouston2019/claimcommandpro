What Is Matching Coverage in Insurance?

When the insurer wants to replace only the damaged portion of your roof or siding, matching coverage may require them to replace the whole thing. Here is how to enforce it.

Definition
Matching coverage requires the insurer to replace an entire contiguous surface — roof, siding, or flooring — when the damaged portion cannot be aesthetically matched to the undamaged portion.

Why Matching Matters

When only a portion of a roof, siding, or floor is damaged, replacing only the damaged section often results in a visible mismatch — different color, different texture, or discontinued materials. Most standard homeowners policies include language requiring the insurer to pay for replacement of the entire surface when matching is not achievable.

Where Matching Claims Are Most Common

  • Roofing — storm damage to one slope when other slopes are undamaged but the shingle is discontinued or faded
  • Siding — impact damage to one wall when surrounding siding cannot be color-matched
  • Flooring — water damage to one room when the flooring runs continuously through adjacent rooms
  • Brick and masonry — when replacement brick does not match existing brick color or texture

State Matching Laws

Many states have adopted explicit matching laws or regulations that require insurers to pay for matching replacement. Even in states without explicit statutes, most standard policy language includes a "like kind and quality" requirement that supports matching claims. Document the mismatch with photographs and a written contractor statement.

Insurers frequently attempt to pay only for damaged sections when matching is required. The documented response is a written supplement request with contractor confirmation that matching materials are unavailable — and a reference to your state's matching law if applicable.

How to Document a Matching Claim

  • Photograph the damaged area next to the undamaged area showing the visible difference
  • Get a written statement from your contractor confirming matching materials are unavailable or that visible mismatch would result from partial replacement
  • Research your state's matching law or regulation
  • Submit a supplement request citing the like kind and quality provision in your policy
