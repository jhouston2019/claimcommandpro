How Does a Hail Claim Work for a Roof?

Hail roof claims are disputed more than any other claim type. Here is the process — and where most policyholders lose money.

Definition
A roof hail claim is the process of documenting, submitting, and negotiating compensation for hail impact damage to roofing materials — requiring storm documentation, damage assessment, and comparison to the insurer's estimate.

Step-by-Step Roof Hail Claim Process

  • Document the storm — get a certified storm report confirming hail date, size, and location
  • Photograph all damage — per-slope photos showing impact patterns and granule loss
  • Get a roofing contractor assessment identifying damage per roof plane and components
  • File the claim and schedule adjuster inspection
  • Be present for the inspection and walk every slope with the adjuster
  • Obtain the adjuster's estimate and compare it to your contractor's estimate line by line
  • Submit supplement for any missing items

What Roof Hail Estimates Most Commonly Miss

  • Drip edge — code required in most states
  • Ice and water shield — required in cold climate zones
  • Ridge vent — when hail damaged the existing vent
  • O&P — general contractor management cost
  • Permits — required for every replacement
  • Matching — when only damaged slopes are priced

The adjuster's hail estimate is the starting point — not the settlement. A line-item comparison between the adjuster's estimate and your roofing contractor's estimate is the most effective supplement tool.
