Settlement Terms

What Is Underpayment in an Insurance Claim?

Underpayment is the norm on disputed property claims — not the exception. Here is how to identify it and what to do about it.

Definition
Insurance underpayment is the difference between what a policyholder is entitled to receive under their policy for a covered loss and what the insurer actually paid — resulting from pricing suppression, scope omissions, improper depreciation, or missed coverage.

How Underpayment Happens

  • Labor rate suppression — insurer prices labor below local market rates
  • Scope omissions — damage present but not included in the estimate
  • O&P omitted — general contractor management cost not included
  • Improper depreciation — non-depreciable items reduced
  • Code upgrade omissions — required improvements not included
  • Missed endorsements — coverage exists but was not applied

How to Identify Underpayment

Compare the insurer's estimate to an independent contractor estimate line by line. Every variance is a potential underpayment. Calculate the total gap. That number is your documented claim.

How to Recover Underpayment

  • Submit a supplement request with contractor documentation
  • Send a demand letter citing specific policy language
  • Invoke the appraisal clause if negotiation fails
  • File a Department of Insurance complaint if conduct is unreasonable

The average documented underpayment on disputed property claims is $18,400. That number is not a marketing claim — it is the arithmetic difference between insurer pricing and contractor pricing on the same scope of work.
