What Is Mitigation in an Insurance Claim?

Your policy requires you to mitigate — and pays for it. Here is what that means and how to document mitigation costs correctly.

Definition
Mitigation is the obligation to take prompt and reasonable action to prevent further damage after a covered loss — and the corresponding coverage for the reasonable costs of doing so.

What Mitigation Requires

  • Tarp a damaged roof to prevent water intrusion
  • Extract standing water to prevent mold and structural damage
  • Board broken windows and doors to prevent additional damage
  • Turn off water supply at the source of a burst pipe
  • Remove contents at risk of further damage

What Mitigation Covers

Your policy covers the reasonable cost of mitigation — the steps required to prevent further damage. Professional water extraction, industrial drying equipment, emergency tarping, and board-up services are standard mitigation costs that are covered under virtually every standard policy. Keep every receipt.

Mitigation vs Permanent Repair

Mitigation is temporary — it stabilizes the property and prevents additional loss. Permanent repair is the full restoration to pre-loss condition. Do not begin permanent repair before the adjuster's inspection. Do begin mitigation immediately after the loss.

Failure to mitigate gives the insurer grounds to deny coverage for damage that occurred after the loss event — damage that reasonable mitigation would have prevented. Document every mitigation action with receipts and photographs.
