Dispute Terms

What Is Insurance Mediation?

Mediation is a faster, cheaper alternative to litigation. Here is when it makes sense and what to expect.

Definition
Insurance mediation is a voluntary dispute resolution process in which a neutral third-party mediator facilitates negotiation between the policyholder and insurer to reach a mutually acceptable settlement.

How Mediation Works

Both parties agree to mediate. A neutral mediator — typically a retired judge or experienced attorney — facilitates discussion. The mediator does not decide the outcome — they help both sides reach agreement. Any settlement reached in mediation is binding if reduced to a signed agreement.

Mediation vs Appraisal vs Litigation

  • Mediation — voluntary, non-binding unless settled, covers both coverage and amount disputes
  • Appraisal — binding, covers amount disputes only, typically faster
  • Litigation — formal legal proceeding, most expensive, longest timeline

When Mediation Makes Sense

Mediation is most useful when both parties are willing to negotiate but cannot reach agreement directly. It is less useful when the insurer has taken a rigid legal position on coverage — in that case, appraisal or litigation may be more appropriate.

Some states require insurers to offer or participate in mediation for property claims above certain thresholds. Check your state's insurance mediation program — it may be free or low cost.
