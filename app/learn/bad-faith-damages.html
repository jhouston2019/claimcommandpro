What Are Bad Faith Damages in Insurance?

When an insurer handles your claim unreasonably, you may be entitled to more than just the claim value. Here is what bad faith damages can include.

Definition
Bad faith damages are compensation awarded to a policyholder beyond the original claim value when an insurer's conduct in handling the claim was unreasonable, dishonest, or in violation of their duty of good faith.

What Bad Faith Damages Can Include

  • The original claim value — what the insurer should have paid
  • Consequential damages — losses resulting from the insurer's delay or denial beyond the claim itself
  • Emotional distress damages — in some jurisdictions
  • Attorney fees — in most bad faith cases
  • Punitive damages — in cases of egregious conduct

What Must Be Proven

To recover bad faith damages, the policyholder typically must prove that the claim was covered under the policy, that the insurer's conduct was unreasonable — not just wrong — and that the conduct caused harm beyond the original claim.

State Law Variation

Bad faith law varies significantly by state. Some states have explicit bad faith statutes with statutory damages. Others rely on common law bad faith principles. The availability and scope of bad faith damages depends on your state's law.

Bad faith litigation is separate from and in addition to your underlying claim dispute. Consult a policyholder attorney before pursuing bad faith damages — the standard of proof is higher than a standard claim dispute.
