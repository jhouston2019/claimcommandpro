Loss Type Terms

What Is a Total Loss in Property Insurance?

A total loss triggers different coverage rules than a partial loss. Here is how it is determined — and what you are actually entitled to when it happens.

Definition
A total loss in property insurance occurs when the cost to repair or rebuild the damaged property exceeds its insured value or a statutory threshold — triggering full replacement cost payment rather than partial repair.

How Total Loss Is Determined

Total loss thresholds vary by state. Most states define total loss as damage exceeding 50 to 80 percent of the property's insured value — the specific percentage is set by state law or policy terms. When damage exceeds the threshold, the property is declared a total loss and full replacement cost is owed rather than repair cost.

What You Are Entitled To

  • Full replacement cost if you have RCV coverage — the cost to rebuild to like kind and quality
  • ACV if you have ACV coverage — the depreciated market value
  • Extended replacement cost above the dwelling limit if you have that endorsement
  • Additional living expenses during reconstruction
  • Code upgrade costs if you have ordinance and law coverage

Disputing a Total Loss Determination

If the insurer declares total loss when you believe repair is feasible and within limits, get an independent contractor estimate for repair versus rebuild and challenge the determination with documentation.

On a total loss, the rebuild cost calculation matters enormously. If current construction costs exceed your dwelling limit, extended replacement cost coverage bridges the gap. Confirm whether you have it before accepting any total loss settlement.
