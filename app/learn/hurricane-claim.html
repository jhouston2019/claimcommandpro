Loss Type Terms

What Is a Hurricane Insurance Claim?

Hurricane claims involve the hardest coverage distinction in property insurance — wind damage is covered, flood damage requires a separate policy.

Definition
A hurricane insurance claim is a property claim for damage caused by hurricane winds — covered under a standard homeowners policy — requiring careful documentation to distinguish covered wind damage from excluded flood damage.

The Wind vs Flood Line

This is the defining challenge in hurricane claims. Standard homeowners policies cover wind damage — not flood. Flood requires a separate National Flood Insurance Program or private flood policy. When a hurricane causes both wind and flood damage, the claim must separate wind-caused from flood-caused damage — often the most disputed issue in the claim.

What Hurricane Coverage Includes

  • Wind damage to roof, walls, windows, and structure
  • Wind-driven rain that enters through wind-created openings
  • Storm surge damage — in states with hurricane deductibles and specific wind-water separation rules
  • Damage from wind-displaced objects and debris

Hurricane Deductibles

Many coastal policies have separate hurricane deductibles — typically 2 to 5 percent of the dwelling limit — that apply instead of the standard deductible when a named storm triggers the coverage. On a $400,000 home, a 2 percent hurricane deductible is $8,000. Confirm your hurricane deductible before calculating your expected payment.

Document wind damage separately from flood damage with photographs taken before water recedes. A contractor's written report attributing specific damage to wind versus flood is essential in a combined event.
