What Is a Declarations Page in Insurance?

The declarations page is the most important page in your policy. Here is how to read every line — and what to look for before filing a claim.

Definition
The declarations page is a summary document at the front of your insurance policy that lists your coverage limits, deductible amounts, premium, policy period, named insured, property address, and active endorsements.

What the Declarations Page Contains

  • Named insured — who is covered under the policy
  • Property address — the insured location
  • Policy period — effective and expiration dates
  • Coverage A limit — dwelling coverage amount
  • Coverage B limit — other structures amount
  • Coverage C limit — personal property amount
  • Coverage D limit — loss of use amount
  • Deductible — the amount you pay before insurance applies
  • Settlement type — RCV or ACV for each coverage
  • Active endorsements — listed by form number and description
  • Premium — what you pay for the policy

How to Read Coverage Limits

Each coverage line shows a dollar amount — the maximum the insurer will pay for that category of loss. Coverage A should reflect the cost to rebuild your home, not its market value. Coverage C is typically 50 to 70 percent of Coverage A. Coverage D is typically 20 to 30 percent of Coverage A. Coverage B is typically 10 percent of Coverage A.

What to Check Before Filing a Claim

  • Confirm the policy is in force — check the policy period
  • Confirm the named insured is correct
  • Note your deductible — it applies before any payment
  • Note your settlement type — RCV or ACV
  • List all active endorsements — these modify your coverage
  • Confirm your Coverage A limit reflects current rebuild cost

The declarations page lists endorsements by form number only — not a full description. You must read the full endorsement text in the body of your policy to understand what coverage it adds or removes. Do not assume you know what an endorsement does from the form number alone.
