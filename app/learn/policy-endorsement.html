Coverage Terms

What Is a Policy Endorsement?

Endorsements are where your most valuable coverages often hide. Here is what they are, how they work, and which ones to look for before accepting a settlement.

Definition
A policy endorsement is a written modification to your base insurance policy that adds, removes, or changes specific coverage terms — superseding the base policy language where they conflict.

How Endorsements Work

Your base homeowners policy establishes default coverage terms. Endorsements modify those terms for your specific situation. An endorsement always takes precedence over the base policy language it modifies. When reviewing your policy for a claim, you must read both the base policy and all endorsements — the endorsement may provide coverage the base policy does not.

Endorsements That Most Affect Claim Value

  • Ordinance and law — adds code upgrade coverage not in the base policy
  • Extended replacement cost — increases the dwelling limit above Coverage A
  • Water backup and sewer — adds coverage for drain and sump pump backup
  • Scheduled personal property — adds coverage above sublimits for jewelry, art, collectibles
  • Equipment breakdown — covers mechanical failure of home systems
  • Identity theft — typically not relevant to property claims

Where to Find Your Endorsements

Endorsements are listed on your declarations page with a form number and a brief description. The full endorsement text is in a separate section of your policy document following the base policy language. Read every endorsement that is listed — even those that seem unrelated to your loss may contain relevant coverage modifications.

Adjusters frequently price claims against the base policy without reading endorsements. If you have an ordinance and law or extended replacement cost endorsement that was not applied to your estimate, you are owed the difference.
