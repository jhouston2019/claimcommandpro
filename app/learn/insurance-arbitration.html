Dispute Terms

What Is Insurance Arbitration?

Arbitration is binding — an arbitrator decides the outcome, not a judge or jury. Here is when it applies in property insurance and what to expect.

Definition
Insurance arbitration is a binding dispute resolution process in which a neutral arbitrator or panel hears evidence from both parties and issues a decision that is typically final and legally enforceable.

Arbitration vs Mediation vs Appraisal

  • Mediation — facilitates negotiation, non-binding unless settled
  • Arbitration — arbitrator decides, binding decision
  • Appraisal — appraisers determine loss amount only, binding

When Arbitration Applies in Property Claims

Some insurance policies include mandatory arbitration clauses for certain disputes. Some states have arbitration programs for property claim disputes. Unlike appraisal — which is limited to amount disputes — arbitration can resolve coverage disputes as well.

What to Know Before Arbitrating

  • Review your policy for arbitration clauses
  • Understand whether arbitration is mandatory or voluntary under your policy
  • Arbitration decisions are typically final and have limited appeal rights
  • Discovery and evidence rules are more informal than court
  • Legal representation is strongly recommended

If your policy contains a mandatory arbitration clause, you may waive your right to sue in court by accepting coverage under the policy. Read the clause carefully before initiating any dispute.
