Estimate Terms

What Is Labor Rate Suppression in Insurance?

If your insurer's estimate uses labor rates 15 to 30 percent below what local contractors charge, that gap is documented and disputable. Here is how it works.

Definition
Labor rate suppression is the practice of pricing labor in an insurance estimate at rates below current local market wages — resulting in an estimate that cannot be executed at the priced rates in the local labor market.

How Labor Rate Suppression Works

Xactimate and other estimating platforms set labor rates based on regional averages from historical data. In high-cost markets, in post-disaster conditions, or when skilled labor is scarce, these regional averages significantly understate what contractors actually charge. An insurer using Xactimate's default labor rates in a market where contractors charge 25 percent more is suppressing your labor costs by that margin.

Where Labor Rate Suppression Appears

  • Roofing labor — per square rates below local contractor quotes
  • Drywall and painting — per square foot rates below market
  • Framing and carpentry — hourly rates below prevailing wages
  • Electrical and plumbing — rates below licensed contractor pricing

How to Document It

Get two or three contractor quotes for the same scope of work. If all of them price labor above the insurer's rates, you have documented evidence of suppression. Compile the quotes and submit a supplement request showing the variance between the insurer's labor rates and market rates.

Labor rate suppression is not accidental. Insurers know their Xactimate rates lag the market. Challenging labor rates with documented contractor quotes is the most effective way to recover suppressed labor costs.
