Coverage Terms

What Is an Open Perils Policy?

Open perils means everything is covered unless the policy specifically says otherwise. Here is how that shifts the burden to the insurer — and how to use it.

Definition
An open perils policy covers all causes of loss to the insured property except those specifically excluded by name — placing the burden on the insurer to prove an exclusion applies rather than on the policyholder to prove a peril is listed.

How Open Perils Coverage Works

Under an open perils structure, your property is covered against all causes of loss. If the insurer wants to deny your claim, they must identify a specific exclusion that applies to your cause of loss and prove that it applies. You do not need to prove your cause of loss is listed — the default is coverage.

Open Perils vs Named Perils — The Burden Shift

This is the critical practical difference between open and named perils. Under named perils, you must prove your loss falls within a listed category. Under open perils, the insurer must prove your loss falls within an exclusion. Open perils provides broader protection and stronger claim leverage.

Where Open Perils Applies in a Standard HO-3

  • Coverage A (dwelling) — open perils
  • Coverage B (other structures) — open perils
  • Coverage C (personal property) — named perils in most HO-3 policies
  • HO-5 policies — open perils on both dwelling and personal property

When an insurer denies a claim under an open perils policy, they must cite the specific exclusion language and explain how it applies to your cause of loss. A denial without a specific exclusion citation is not legally sufficient.
