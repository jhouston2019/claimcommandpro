What Is a Reservation of Rights Letter?

A reservation of rights letter is not a denial. Here is what it actually means — and what you need to do when you receive one.

Definition
A reservation of rights letter is a notice from an insurer that they are handling a claim while reserving their right to deny coverage based on specific policy provisions — typically because coverage questions are under investigation.

What a Reservation of Rights Means

When an insurer sends a reservation of rights letter, they are saying: we will handle your claim while we investigate whether it is covered, but we are not conceding that it is covered. It is a procedural notice, not a denial. Many claims that receive reservation of rights letters are ultimately paid in full.

Why Insurers Send Them

  • The cause of loss is under investigation
  • There is a potential coverage exclusion under review
  • The scope of damage overlaps covered and potentially uncovered causes
  • There is a late notice issue being evaluated
  • A third-party liability question is being investigated

How to Respond

  • Respond in writing acknowledging receipt
  • Do not ignore it — failing to respond can create problems later
  • Request the specific coverage questions being investigated
  • Continue documenting and submitting your claim normally
  • Do not sign any releases until coverage is confirmed

A reservation of rights letter starts a clock. If the insurer does not resolve the coverage question within a reasonable time, their continued handling of your claim while reserving rights may create an estoppel argument in your favor. Document the date you received the letter and every subsequent contact.
