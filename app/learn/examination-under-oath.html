What Is an Examination Under Oath (EUO)?

An examination under oath is a formal proceeding — not a casual interview. Here is what to expect and how to prepare before you agree to one.

Definition
An examination under oath (EUO) is a formal recorded interview conducted by an insurer's attorney in which a policyholder answers questions about their claim under oath — typically in complex, high-value, or suspected fraud cases.

When Insurers Request an EUO

  • High-value claims that exceed normal settlement thresholds
  • Claims where the cause of loss is disputed or unclear
  • Suspected fraud or material misrepresentation
  • Complex total loss situations
  • Claims where prior statements are inconsistent

Your Rights During an EUO

  • You have the right to have an attorney present
  • You have the right to review the transcript
  • You have the right to correct errors in the transcript
  • You must answer truthfully — false statements under oath are perjury
  • Your attorney can object to improper questions

How to Prepare

Do not attend an EUO without legal counsel or at minimum thorough preparation. Review all your claim documentation before the EUO. Know your dates, your claim numbers, and the specific facts of your loss. Stick to what you know — if you do not know something, say so.

An EUO is a formal legal proceeding. The insurer's attorney is not your friend and is looking for inconsistencies that support a denial. Consulting a policyholder attorney before attending is strongly recommended.
