What Is Scope of Loss in Insurance?

The scope of loss determines what gets repaired and what gets paid. Here is how it is defined, who controls it, and what to do when yours is incomplete.

Definition
The scope of loss is a comprehensive description of all property damaged in a covered event — the foundation document from which repair estimates and claim payments are calculated.

What the Scope of Loss Includes

  • Every damaged structural element with dimensions
  • Every damaged system — electrical, plumbing, HVAC
  • Every damaged personal property item
  • The cause of each damage item linked to the covered event
  • Code upgrade requirements triggered by the repair
  • Temporary repair and mitigation work performed

Who Determines the Scope

The insurer's adjuster determines the initial scope during their inspection. This scope becomes the basis for the estimate. The adjuster's scope is not final — it is their documented assessment of the damage. You have the right to supplement the scope with additional items the adjuster missed.

When the Scope Is Incomplete

An incomplete scope is the most common cause of underpayment. If the adjuster did not inspect all areas, did not identify hidden damage, or did not include code-required work, the scope is deficient. Your contractor's scope — based on a complete assessment — is the counter.

The adjuster's scope is prepared in a single inspection visit. Your contractor has unlimited time to assess the damage before producing their scope. A contractor's comprehensive scope is almost always more complete than an adjuster's first inspection scope.
