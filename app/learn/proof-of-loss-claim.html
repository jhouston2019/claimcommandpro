What Is a Proof of Loss?

The proof of loss is the most important document you submit in your claim. Here is what it is, when it is due, and how to make sure it works for you — not against you.

Definition
A proof of loss is a sworn, signed statement submitted by the policyholder to the insurer specifying the date and cause of loss, the damaged property, and the total dollar amount being claimed.

Proof of Loss vs Initial Claim Report

The initial claim report is a phone call to notify your insurer a loss occurred. The proof of loss is a formal legal document submitted weeks later — after you have documented the damage, obtained contractor estimates, and assembled your evidence. They are not the same document and they serve different legal purposes.

What the Proof of Loss Contains

  • Date and cause of loss
  • Detailed description of all damaged property
  • Total dollar amount claimed
  • List of all attached supporting documents
  • Your policy number and claim number
  • Your sworn signature

Critical Rules

  • Never submit before obtaining your independent contractor estimate
  • Never understate the amount to seem reasonable
  • Always submit via certified mail with return receipt
  • Meet the deadline — typically 60 days from loss or from request
  • Keep a copy of everything submitted

The amount on your proof of loss becomes the ceiling of your claim. If you understate your loss to avoid conflict, you limit your own recovery. Claim the full documented amount.
