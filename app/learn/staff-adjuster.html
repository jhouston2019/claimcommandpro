Claim Process Terms

What Is a Staff Adjuster?

Your staff adjuster works for the insurer — not for you. Here is what that means for how they approach your claim.

Definition
A staff adjuster is a licensed claims professional employed directly by an insurance company to investigate, evaluate, and settle claims on behalf of the insurer.

What Staff Adjusters Do

  • Receive and open claims reported by policyholders
  • Schedule and conduct property inspections
  • Prepare damage estimates using insurer-approved estimating software
  • Issue coverage determinations
  • Negotiate and settle claims within their authority limit
  • Escalate complex claims to supervisors or specialist units

Staff Adjuster vs Independent Adjuster

Staff adjusters are employees with full access to the insurer's systems, escalation paths, and claims authority. Independent adjusters are contractors with more limited authority who typically must refer coverage decisions to the insurer's internal team. For complex disputes, escalating to a staff adjuster's supervisor is often more effective than working with an independent adjuster.

The Conflict of Interest

Staff adjusters are employees whose performance is measured in part by claim costs. Their incentive is to settle claims accurately but efficiently — which in practice means at the lowest defensible number. They are not your advocate and are not required to volunteer coverage information you do not ask for.

Ask your staff adjuster for their supervisor's name and contact information at the start of your claim. If your adjuster is unresponsive or unreasonable, escalating to their supervisor in writing is your most effective first escalation step.
