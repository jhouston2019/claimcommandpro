Coverage Terms

What Is Dwelling Coverage (Coverage A)?

Coverage A is the foundation of your homeowners policy. Here is what it covers, how the limit is determined, and what adjusters most commonly get wrong.

Definition
Dwelling coverage (Coverage A) pays to repair or rebuild the physical structure of your home — walls, roof, floors, built-in appliances, and attached structures — after a covered loss.

What Coverage A Pays For

  • Structural repairs to walls, roof, floors, and foundation
  • Attached structures — garage, deck, porch, and sunroom
  • Built-in appliances — HVAC, water heater, built-in dishwasher
  • Electrical, plumbing, and HVAC systems within the dwelling
  • Windows, doors, and permanently installed fixtures

What Coverage A Does Not Pay For

  • Detached structures — covered under Coverage B
  • Personal property — covered under Coverage C
  • Additional living expenses — covered under Coverage D
  • Flood damage — requires separate flood policy
  • Earthquake damage — requires separate endorsement
  • Normal wear and tear or maintenance issues

How the Coverage A Limit Is Set

The dwelling limit should reflect the cost to rebuild your home from the ground up — not its market value. Market value includes land, which cannot be destroyed. Replacement cost is the cost of labor and materials to reconstruct the structure. These numbers can differ significantly. Underinsurance — having a dwelling limit below actual rebuild cost — is one of the most common and costly insurance mistakes.

The most common Coverage A error is underinsurance — a dwelling limit set below the actual cost to rebuild. Construction costs increase over time. If you have not reviewed your dwelling limit in more than two years, it may be insufficient.
