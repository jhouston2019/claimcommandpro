What Is a Claim Number in Insurance?

Your claim number is the key to everything. Here is why it matters, when you should have it, and what every piece of correspondence needs to include.

Definition
A claim number is the unique alphanumeric identifier assigned by your insurer when you report a loss — used to track every document, payment, and communication related to your claim.

When You Should Receive a Claim Number

Most insurers assign a claim number during or immediately after the first notice of loss call. If you do not receive one on the call, request it by email the same day. A claim number confirms the insurer has officially opened your claim — the absence of one is a red flag.

Why It Matters

  • Every document you submit should include your claim number
  • Every letter to your insurer should reference your claim number
  • Your adjuster locates your file by claim number
  • Regulatory complaints reference your claim number
  • Payment records are tracked by claim number

What to Do If You Cannot Get One

If the insurer fails to assign a claim number within 24 hours, send a written notice via certified mail confirming your loss report and requesting a claim number in writing. Document the date and time of your original report. If no claim number is assigned within 5 business days, file a complaint with your state's Department of Insurance.

Write your claim number on every document you send. Include it in every email subject line. Reference it in every phone call. A claim number ensures your documentation reaches your file — not someone else's.
