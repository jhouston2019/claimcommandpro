Coverage Terms

What Is Extended Replacement Cost Coverage?

If construction costs spike after a major loss, your dwelling limit may not be enough. Extended replacement cost is the coverage that fills the gap.

Definition
Extended replacement cost coverage increases your dwelling limit by a specified percentage — typically 25 to 50 percent — when the actual cost to rebuild exceeds your policy's Coverage A limit.

Why Dwelling Limits Become Insufficient

Construction costs fluctuate — materials and labor prices change significantly over time and spike after major regional disasters when demand for contractors exceeds supply. A dwelling limit set at policy inception may be insufficient to cover actual rebuilding costs years later, especially after inflation and post-disaster price increases.

How Extended Replacement Cost Works

Extended replacement cost is an endorsement that adds a buffer above your Coverage A limit. If your dwelling limit is $300,000 and you have 25 percent extended replacement cost, your effective coverage is $375,000. If rebuilding costs $360,000, the endorsement covers the $60,000 above your base limit.

Guaranteed vs Extended Replacement Cost

  • Extended replacement cost — adds a fixed percentage above the dwelling limit (25 percent or 50 percent are common)
  • Guaranteed replacement cost — pays whatever rebuilding actually costs regardless of the dwelling limit. Rare and typically more expensive.

Extended replacement cost does not automatically increase over time. If construction costs have risen significantly since you set your dwelling limit, the endorsement buffer may not be sufficient. Review your coverage limit annually.

How to Confirm You Have It

Check your declarations page for "Extended Replacement Cost," "ERC," or "Additional Replacement Cost" with a percentage listed. Also check endorsements — it is sometimes added as a separate endorsement rather than appearing on the declarations page.
