Coverage Terms

What Is a Named Perils Policy?

Named perils means the burden is on you to prove your loss was caused by something the policy lists. Here is what that means in practice.

Definition
A named perils policy covers only the specific causes of loss listed by name in the policy — if your cause of loss is not on the list, it is not covered.

Named Perils vs Open Perils

Named perils and open perils are the two fundamental coverage structures in property insurance. A named perils policy lists every covered cause of loss — fire, lightning, windstorm, hail, explosion, and others. If your damage was caused by something not on that list, it is not covered. An open perils policy covers everything except what is specifically excluded — a much broader protection.

Standard Named Perils in HO-3 Policies

The HO-3 — the most common homeowners policy — uses a hybrid structure. The dwelling (Coverage A) is covered on an open perils basis. Personal property (Coverage C) is typically covered on a named perils basis. The standard named perils for Coverage C include fire, lightning, windstorm, hail, explosion, riot, aircraft, vehicles, smoke, vandalism, theft, falling objects, weight of ice or snow, water discharge from plumbing, and electrical damage.

How Named Perils Affects Your Claim

Under a named perils structure, the burden is on the policyholder to demonstrate that the damage was caused by a listed peril. Documenting the cause of loss is therefore critical. A contractor report attributing damage to a specific named peril is stronger evidence than a general description of damage.

If your contents were damaged and your policy covers contents on a named perils basis, your claim documentation must identify which named peril caused the damage. A water damage claim must be linked to one of the water-related named perils — not just described as water damage.
