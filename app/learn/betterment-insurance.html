What Is Betterment in an Insurance Claim?

Insurers use betterment to reduce claims when they argue repairs improve your property. Here is when that argument is valid — and when it is not.

Definition
Betterment is a reduction applied to an insurance claim when a repair or replacement improves the property beyond its pre-loss condition — the policyholder is expected to contribute the portion representing the improvement.

When Betterment Applies

Betterment applies when the repair genuinely upgrades the property. If your 15-year-old carpet is destroyed and you replace it with higher-end carpet than you had, the insurer can apply betterment for the difference. If you had standard shingles and you upgrade to architectural shingles, betterment may apply to the difference.

When Betterment Does Not Apply

Betterment does not apply when code requires an upgrade — if current building codes require the better material, the cost is covered under ordinance and law coverage, not reduced as betterment. It also does not apply when the upgraded material is the only like-kind-and-quality option currently available — if the original product is discontinued, the replacement is not betterment.

How to Dispute Improper Betterment

  • Confirm the upgrade was code-required — if so, ordinance and law coverage applies
  • Confirm the original product is available — if not, the replacement is not betterment
  • Request the insurer's specific betterment calculation and the basis for it
  • Challenge betterment applied to code-required upgrades with an ordinance and law coverage citation

Insurers sometimes apply betterment to every upgrade regardless of whether it is code-required. Code-required upgrades are not betterment — they are compliance costs covered under ordinance and law. The two are frequently confused.
