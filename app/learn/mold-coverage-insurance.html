What Is Mold Coverage in Homeowners Insurance?

Mold coverage depends entirely on what caused the moisture — and whether you mitigated promptly. Here is what is covered and what is not.

Definition
Mold coverage in a standard homeowners policy pays for mold remediation when mold results directly from a covered sudden water event and mitigation was performed promptly — but excludes mold from gradual leaks or neglect.

When Mold Is Covered

Mold is covered when it results from a covered water event — a burst pipe, storm damage, or appliance failure — and when the policyholder took prompt mitigation action. If mold forms because water was not extracted and dried within 24 to 48 hours, the insurer may dispute coverage on the basis that the policyholder failed to mitigate.

When Mold Is Not Covered

  • Mold from gradual leaks excluded as maintenance issue
  • Mold pre-dating the covered event
  • Mold resulting from failure to mitigate the covered event
  • Stand-alone mold policies or endorsements may be needed for mold not resulting from a covered peril

How to Document Mold for Coverage

  • Document the covered water event that caused the moisture
  • Document mitigation response timeline — extraction, drying equipment, and drying logs
  • Have a mold remediation contractor assess and document the extent of mold
  • Link the mold growth to the specific covered water event

Drying logs from your mitigation contractor are the most important document in a mold coverage dispute. They prove prompt response — undermining any argument that the mold resulted from your failure to act.
