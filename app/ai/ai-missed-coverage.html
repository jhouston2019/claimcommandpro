How Coverage Gets Missed

Insurance adjusters process claims quickly. They apply the coverages that are obvious and standard. Endorsements that modify base coverage, matching clauses that require full surface replacement, ordinance and law provisions that pay for code upgrades, and extended replacement cost provisions that increase the dwelling limit — these are frequently overlooked because they require careful policy reading that field adjusters rarely have time for.

Coverages AI Most Frequently Finds Missing

  • Ordinance and law coverage — pays for building code upgrade costs during repair
  • Extended replacement cost — adds 25 to 50 percent above dwelling limit
  • Matching clause — requires full surface replacement when damaged sections cannot match
  • Recoverable depreciation — RCV holdback not released after repairs
  • Contents replacement cost — ACV applied when RCV endorsement exists
  • Business use endorsement — coverage for home office equipment
  • Scheduled property endorsements — specific high-value items with separate limits

How AI Coverage Gap Detection Works

Upload your policy and describe your claim. AI cross-references every coverage section, endorsement, and condition against your reported loss type and the insurer's estimate. For each coverage that exists in your policy but was not applied in the estimate, AI produces a written coverage citation you can submit to your insurer demanding application.

The most valuable missed coverages — ordinance and law, extended replacement cost, and matching — are also the most commonly ignored. On a $50,000 claim, these three alone can add $10,000 to $20,000 in legitimate additional coverage.
