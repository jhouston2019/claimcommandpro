Why Fire Claims Are Complex

Fire claims involve more coverage types simultaneously than any other loss — dwelling structure, contents, additional living expenses, smoke damage, demolition, code upgrades, and sometimes total loss calculation. Each requires separate documentation. Missing documentation in any one area reduces the corresponding payment.

What AI Fire Claim Review Covers

  • Dwelling structure — scope completeness and pricing accuracy
  • Contents inventory — every item documented with replacement value
  • Smoke and soot damage — separate scope from fire damage
  • ALE — all displacement expenses documented and claimed
  • Demolition and debris removal — included in estimate
  • Code upgrades — ordinance and law coverage applied if available
  • Total loss calculation — replacement cost versus actual cash value
  • Extended replacement cost — applied if endorsement exists

Contents Inventory After a Fire

A complete contents inventory after a fire is one of the most time-consuming tasks in a property claim. Every item must be listed with description, age, brand, condition, and replacement value. AI helps structure the inventory room by room and identifies categories of items commonly omitted — clothing, garage contents, food, medications, electronics accessories, and seasonal items.

Contents are the most undervalued component of fire claims. Most policyholders underestimate the replacement value of their belongings by 40 to 60 percent. A room-by-room AI-assisted inventory closes that gap.
